Module 4Subspaces, bases and dimension

1

Closure and span

A subspace of Rn\mathbb R^nRn contains zero and is closed under addition and scalar multiplication. An equivalent test for a nonempty set is closure under every linear combination of two of its members. This explains why the line y=2xy=2xy=2x is a subspace and the parallel line y=2x+1y=2x+1y=2x+1 is not. The latter misses zero.

The span of a list of vectors consists of all their linear combinations. It is a subspace because adding or scaling combinations gives another combination of the same list. In particular, the span is the smallest subspace containing the list.

Change the generating vectors. Compare the two directions when they are independent with what happens when one is a multiple of the other.

2

Independent directions and coordinates

A list is linearly independent when the zero vector has only one representation as a combination of the list, with all coefficients zero. Otherwise at least one vector is redundant: solve a nonzero relation for any vector with a nonzero coefficient.

A basis is an independent spanning list. Spanning gives coordinates for every vector. Independence makes those coordinates unique, because two different coordinate lists for the same vector would yield a nonzero relation on subtraction. The dimension is the number of vectors in a basis. One way to justify that the count is fixed: a basis of kkk vectors identifies the space with Rk,\mathbb R^k\mathclose{\text{,}}Rk, where more than kkk vectors are dependent by elimination and fewer than kkk cannot span because their matrix has fewer than kkk pivots.

Example 4.1A basis in a plane✓ Checked

In R3\mathbb R^3R3 take the plane x+y+z=0.x+y+z=0\mathclose{\text{.}}x+y+z=0. Put x=s,x=s\mathclose{\text{,}}x=s, y=ty=ty=t and then z=−s−t.z=-s-t\mathclose{\text{.}}z=−s−t. The vectors (1,0,−1)T(1,0,-1)^\mathsf T(1,0,−1)T and (0,1,−1)T(0,1,-1)^\mathsf T(0,1,−1)T span the plane. Comparing their first two entries proves independence. The dimension is 2.2\mathclose{\text{.}}2.

Change the basis, keep the vector

Put the basis columns into B.B\mathclose{\text{.}}B. If c\mathbf cc is the coordinate vector relative to that ordered basis, then BcB\mathbf cBc is the vector in ordinary coordinates. To find c,\mathbf c\mathclose{\text{,}}c, solve Bc=v.B\mathbf c=\mathbf v\mathclose{\text{.}}Bc=v. The same physical displacement can have different coordinates in different bases.

Compute itCoordinates in a nonstandard basis
Python
from sympy import Matrix

B = Matrix([[1, 1], [0, 1]])
v = Matrix([5, 2])
print(list(B.LUsolve(v)))
Output
[3, 2]

Exercise 4.2★★Remove a redundant generator

Find a basis for the span of (1,0,1)T,(1,0,1)^\mathsf T\mathclose{\text{,}}(1,0,1)T, (0,1,1)T,(0,1,1)^\mathsf T\mathclose{\text{,}}(0,1,1)T, and (1,1,2)T.(1,1,2)^\mathsf T\mathclose{\text{.}}(1,1,2)T.

Show solution✓ Checked

The third is the sum of the first two, so it adds no direction. The first two are independent because their first two entries form the coordinate vectors of R2.\mathbb R^2\mathclose{\text{.}}R2. Keep those two.

Exercise 4.3★★★Coordinates in two bases

Find the coordinates of (5,2)T(5,2)^\mathsf T(5,2)T relative to the ordered basis (1,0)T,(1,1)T,(1,0)^\mathsf T,(1,1)^\mathsf T\mathclose{\text{,}}(1,0)T,(1,1)T, then relative to the reversed order.

Show solution✓ Checked

In the first order, the second entry gives coefficient 222 for (1,1)T.(1,1)^\mathsf T\mathclose{\text{.}}(1,1)T. The first entry leaves coefficient 333 for (1,0)T.(1,0)^\mathsf T\mathclose{\text{.}}(1,0)T. Reversing the order gives coordinates (2,3)T.(2,3)^\mathsf T\mathclose{\text{.}}(2,3)T.
