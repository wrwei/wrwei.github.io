The closest reachable observation

Suppose b\mathbf bb consists of measurements and AxA\mathbf xAx is the vector of predictions. There may be no exact solution. Least squares minimises ∥Ax−b∥2.\|A\mathbf x-\mathbf b\|^2\mathclose{\text{.}}∥Ax−b∥2. The fitted vector is the orthogonal projection of b\mathbf bb onto the column space, because that is where every candidate prediction lies. The residual r=b−Ax^\mathbf r=\mathbf b-A\widehat{\mathbf x}r=b−Ax is orthogonal to every column. This gives the normal equations

ATAx^=ATb.A^\mathsf TA\widehat{\mathbf x}=A^\mathsf T\mathbf b. ATAx=ATb.

Conversely, any solution of these equations is a least-squares solution by the squared-distance decomposition. The fitted vector is unique even if the coefficients are not. Any two coefficient vectors producing the same fit differ by a null-space vector.

Example 8.1Fit a line with intercept✓ Checked

For data (0,1),(1,2),(2,2),(0,1),(1,2),(2,2)\mathclose{\text{,}}(0,1),(1,2),(2,2), use columns of ones and input values, in that order. The normal equations for intercept aaa and slope bbb are 3a+3b=53a+3b=53a+3b=5 and 3a+5b=6.3a+5b=6\mathclose{\text{.}}3a+5b=6. Subtraction gives b=1/2b=1/2b=1/2 and then a=7/6.a=7/6\mathclose{\text{.}}a=7/6. The residuals (−1/6,1/3,−1/6)(-1/6,1/3,-1/6)(−1/6,1/3,−1/6) sum to zero and also have zero dot product with (0,1,2)T.(0,1,2)^\mathsf T\mathclose{\text{.}}(0,1,2)T.

Solve with QR

For independent columns, write A=QRA=QRA=QR and express the projection as QQTb.QQ^\mathsf T\mathbf b\mathclose{\text{.}}QQTb. Matching coefficients in the columns of QQQ gives Rx^=QTb.R\widehat{\mathbf x}=Q^\mathsf T\mathbf b\mathclose{\text{.}}Rx=QTb. This is a triangular solve. It avoids forming ATA,A^\mathsf TA\mathclose{\text{,}}ATA, whose condition number is the square of that of A.A\mathclose{\text{.}}A. Module 11 derives that ratio.

Small perturbations in observations can force a large change in coefficients when the columns are nearly dependent. For A=(110ε),A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&\varepsilon\end{pmatrix}\mathclose{\text{,}}A=(10​1ε​), increasing the second observation by ε\varepsilonε increases the second coefficient by 111 and decreases the first by 1.1\mathclose{\text{.}}1. The prediction has only changed a little. Treat a small residual as evidence about fit to the measurements, and examine sensitivity separately.

Compute itSolve the line fit with QR
Python
from sympy import Matrix

A = Matrix([[1, 0], [1, 1], [1, 2]])
b = Matrix([1, 2, 2])
Q, R = A.QRdecomposition()
x = R.upper_triangular_solve(Q.T * b)
print(list(x))
print(list(A.T * (b - A * x)))
Output
[7/6, 1/2]
[0, 0]
Compute itFit a line to floating-point data

NumPy’s least-squares solver returns the coefficients and a numerical rank. Give the column order a name before interpreting the result. The residual norm measures the size of the fit error in observation coordinates.

Python
import numpy as np

x_data = np.array([0., 1., 2.])
y_data = np.array([1., 2., 2.])
design = np.column_stack((np.ones(x_data.size), x_data))
coefficients, _, rank, _ = np.linalg.lstsq(design, y_data, rcond=None)
residual = y_data - design @ coefficients
print(f"intercept={coefficients[0]:.3f}, slope={coefficients[1]:.3f}")
print(f"rank={rank}, residual norm={np.linalg.norm(residual):.3f}")
Output
intercept=1.167, slope=0.500
rank=2, residual norm=0.408

Exercise 8.1★Best constant

What constant best fits 1,4,71,4,71,4,7 in the least-squares sense?

Show solution✓ Checked

The design matrix is a column of ones. Residual orthogonality says their sum vanishes, so the fitted constant is the mean 4.4\mathclose{\text{.}}4.

Check yourself

1
In an unweighted least-squares fit, what is perpendicular to each design column?
Residual orthogonality expresses that the fit is the closest point in the column space.
2
Can a rank-deficient design have a unique fitted vector?
Coefficients may differ by a null-space vector while producing the same fit.
3
What do you solve after a thin QR factorisation with independent columns?
