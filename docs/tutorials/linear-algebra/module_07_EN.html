Drop the perpendicular

To approximate b\mathbf bb by cuc\mathbf ucu for nonzero u,\mathbf u\mathclose{\text{,}}u, choose the point on the line where the residual is perpendicular to u.\mathbf u\mathclose{\text{.}}u. Solving u⋅(b−cu)=0\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf b-c\mathbf u)=0u⋅(b−cu)=0 gives

c=u⋅bu⋅u.c=\frac{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf b}{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf u}. c=u⋅uu⋅b​.

For any other point of the line, the squared distance to b\mathbf bb is the squared length of the same perpendicular residual plus the squared distance along the line. The Pythagorean theorem makes the perpendicular foot the unique closest point.

The projection is (1,2)T(1,2)^\mathsf T(1,2)T and the residual (2,−1)T.(2,-1)^\mathsf T\mathclose{\text{.}}(2,−1)T. Their dot product is zero.

An orthonormal basis and Gram–Schmidt

A list is orthonormal when its vectors have unit length and distinct vectors are orthogonal. If QQQ stores an orthonormal basis as columns, QTbQ^\mathsf T\mathbf bQTb gives coefficients of the projection. In fact, QTQ=IQ^\mathsf TQ=IQTQ=I and QT(b−QQTb)=0.Q^\mathsf T(\mathbf b-QQ^\mathsf T\mathbf b)=0\mathclose{\text{.}}QT(b−QQTb)=0.

Gram–Schmidt turns independent columns into such a basis. At each step subtract the projection of the next column onto the preceding orthonormal columns. Normalise the residual. Its length is nonzero by independence. The new vectors span the same spaces at each step, since the subtracted vector is in the previous span.

3

The factorisation

For m×nm\times nm×n matrix AAA with independent columns, this procedure gives A=QRA=QRA=QR with QQQ of shape m×nm\times nm×n and RRR upper triangular of shape n×n.n\times n\mathclose{\text{.}}n×n. Column jjj of AAA combines only the first jjj columns of Q.Q\mathclose{\text{.}}Q. The coefficient of the newest direction is the length of the residual. In floating-point work use a library QR routine. It can use orthogonal reflections to avoid loss of orthogonality when columns nearly depend on each other.

Compute itCheck both properties
Python
from sympy import Matrix

A = Matrix([[1, 1], [1, 0], [0, 1]])
Q, R = A.QRdecomposition()
print((Q.T * Q).tolist())
print(Q * R == A)
Output
[[1, 0], [0, 1]]

Check yourself

1
Why is there a denominator in the projection coefficient onto a non-unit vector?
Solving the perpendicularity condition divides by the vector’s dot product with itself.
2
Which identity holds for a tall matrix QQQ with orthonormal columns?
Every inner product of distinct columns vanishes and each squared column norm equals one.
3
What happens in Gram–Schmidt if the next column is in the previous span?
Subtracting its projection removes the whole column, so it cannot be normalised.
