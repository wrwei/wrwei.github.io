Orthogonal eigenvectors

A real symmetric matrix satisfies AT=A.A^\mathsf T=A\mathclose{\text{.}}AT=A. Suppose Au=λuA\mathbf u=\lambda\mathbf uAu=λu and Av=μv.A\mathbf v=\mu\mathbf v\mathclose{\text{.}}Av=μv. Symmetry gives (Au)⋅v=u⋅(Av).(A\mathbf u)\cdot\mathbf v=\mathbf u\cdot(A\mathbf v)\mathclose{\text{.}}(Au)⋅v=u⋅(Av). If λ≠μ,\lambda\ne\mu\mathclose{\text{,}}λ=μ, this forces u⋅v=0.\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v=0\mathclose{\text{.}}u⋅v=0.

The spectral theorem says every real symmetric matrix has an orthonormal basis of real eigenvectors. Here is why there is a first direction. The quantity vTAv\mathbf v^\mathsf TA\mathbf vvTAv attains its maximum at some unit vector on the compact unit sphere. For a unit w\mathbf ww perpendicular to the maximiser v,\mathbf v\mathclose{\text{,}}v, the unit direction (v+tw)/1+t2(\mathbf v+t\mathbf w)/\sqrt{1+t^2}(v+tw)/1+t2​ cannot give a larger value. Expanding and clearing the denominator gives

2t wTAv+t2(wTAw−vTAv)≤0.2t\,\mathbf w^\mathsf TA\mathbf v+t^2(\mathbf w^\mathsf TA\mathbf w-\mathbf v^\mathsf TA\mathbf v)\le 0. 2twTAv+t2(wTAw−vTAv)≤0.

The coefficient of ttt must vanish. Otherwise, sufficiently small ttt with the same sign as that coefficient would make the left side positive. Thus AvA\mathbf vAv is parallel to v.\mathbf v\mathclose{\text{.}}v. Symmetry makes its orthogonal complement invariant. Repeat the argument there until you have an orthonormal basis. With these vectors as columns of Q,Q\mathclose{\text{,}}Q, write A=QΛQT.A=Q\Lambda Q^\mathsf T\mathclose{\text{.}}A=QΛQT.

Shapes in principal coordinates

The quadratic form of AAA is q(x)=xTAx.q(\mathbf x)=\mathbf x^\mathsf TA\mathbf x\mathclose{\text{.}}q(x)=xTAx. In eigenvector coordinates z=QTx\mathbf z=Q^\mathsf T\mathbf xz=QTx it becomes ∑iλizi2.\sum_i\lambda_i z_i^2\mathclose{\text{.}}∑i​λi​zi2​. If every eigenvalue is positive, the form is positive definite. If all are nonnegative, it is positive semidefinite, with a flat direction when one is zero. Eigenvalues of both signs give an indefinite form.
