Signed area and volume

In two dimensions the columns (a,c)T(a,c)^\mathsf T(a,c)T and (b,d)T(b,d)^\mathsf T(b,d)T enclose a parallelogram with signed area ad−bc.ad-bc\mathclose{\text{.}}ad−bc. The magnitude gives the area. The sign records whether the linear map preserves or reverses orientation. In higher dimensions the square-matrix determinant measures the signed volume factor.

Swapping rows reverses its sign, scaling a row scales it by the same factor, and adding a multiple of another row leaves it alone. These rules also follow from the determinant being alternating and linear in each row separately. A triangular matrix has determinant equal to the product of its diagonal entries. Track the row swaps and scalings while reducing.

Example 6.1Reduce without changing the determinant✓ Checked

For A=(2143),A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\4&3\end{pmatrix}\mathclose{\text{,}}A=(24​13​), subtract twice the first row from the second. The resulting triangular matrix has diagonal entries 2,1,2,1\mathclose{\text{,}}2,1, so det⁡A=2.\det A=2\mathclose{\text{.}}detA=2. The map doubles ordinary area and preserves orientation.

Which maps have an inverse?

For an n×nn\times nn×n matrix the following are equivalent: rank n,n\mathclose{\text{,}}n, zero kernel, a unique solution for every right-hand side, and nonzero determinant. Elimination explains the last link: there is a zero diagonal pivot in triangular form precisely when there are fewer than nnn pivots. The inverse A−1A^{-1}A−1 reverses the map. Reducing [A∣I][A\mid I][A∣I] gives [I∣A−1][I\mid A^{-1}][I∣A−1] when the inverse exists.

For a single numerical system, use a solver such as elimination rather than computing the inverse and multiplying by b.\mathbf b\mathclose{\text{.}}b. For repeated solves with the same A,A\mathclose{\text{,}}A, compute a factorisation once.

Triangular solves and LU

For the example, elimination gives

A=LU,L=(1021),U=(2101).A=LU,\qquad L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\2&1\end{pmatrix},\quad U=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}. A=LU,L=(12​01​),U=(20​11​).

The entry 222 in LLL records the elimination multiplier. To solve Ax=b,A\mathbf x=\mathbf b\mathclose{\text{,}}Ax=b, solve Lz=bL\mathbf z=\mathbf bLz=b from the top down, then Ux=zU\mathbf x=\mathbf zUx=z from the bottom up. With row swaps the common convention is PA=LU,PA=LU\mathclose{\text{,}}PA=LU, so use PbP\mathbf bPb on the right of the first solve.
