## Who this is for {#who}

Anyone who has never used Git: students, researchers, writers, designers, and programmers who
have so far got by without it. You do not need to be able to program, and you do not need to have
used a terminal before. If you can type and follow instructions, you can do this.

## What you will be able to do {#what}

- Keep the full history of a project, and go back to any version of it.
- Try out ideas on branches, and merge them in, or throw them away.
- Resolve a merge conflict calmly.
- Keep your work on GitHub, and work on it from more than one computer.
- Propose, review and merge changes with pull requests, alone or with others.
- Fix the usual mistakes, and know which "undo" to use.
- Publish a web page of your own with GitHub Pages.

All along you build one small project, a personal web page, and at the end it is online at your
own address.

## The lessons {#lessons}

Do them in order: each lesson starts where the last one ended. Each takes 15 to 45 minutes.

## How to use these lessons {#how}

- **Type every command yourself**, in your own terminal, and compare what you see with what the
  lesson shows. Every output in this series was produced by running the commands, so yours should
  match, apart from hashes, dates, paths, version numbers, sizes and speeds.
- **Read the boxes.** Steps on GitHub's website and in VS Code are in their own boxes, and under
  most steps an **If something went wrong** box lists the messages beginners meet there.
- **Use the tabs.** Where Windows, macOS and Linux differ, the lesson shows a tab for each, and
  opens your system's tab first.
- **Mark each lesson as done** at the bottom of the page. Your progress shows above and on every
  lesson's menu. It is stored in your browser only.
