#! Modules 17 to 20

## function of two variables
links: 17.1#functions
A rule $f$ that assigns to each ordered pair $(x, y)$ in a set $D$ of the plane, its domain, exactly one real number $f(x, y)$. A function of three variables assigns a number $f(x, y, z)$ to each point of a set in space.

## surface
links: 17.1#graphs 20.6#parametric-surfaces
A two-dimensional shape in space, such as the graph $z = f(x, y)$ of a function of two variables, which has one point above each point of the domain, or a parametric surface $\vb{r}(u, v)$.

## contour lines
links: 17.1#level-curves
Lines on a map joining points at one height above sea level. Isotherms join points of equal temperature, isobars points of equal air pressure.

## level curve
links: 17.1#level-curves
A curve $f(x, y) = k$, for a constant $k$ in the range of $f$. A drawing of level curves for equally spaced values of $k$, labelled with the values, is a contour map of $f$.

## level surface
links: 17.1#level-surfaces
A surface $f(x, y, z) = k$ of a function of three variables, for a constant $k$.

## limit in two variables
links: 17.2#limits
For $f$ defined on a disc around $(a, b)$, except perhaps at $(a, b)$: $\lim_{(x, y)\to(a, b)} f(x, y) = L$ if for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a $\delta > 0$ with $\abs{f(x, y) - L} < \varepsilon$ for every $(x, y)$ at distance between 0 and $\delta$ from $(a, b)$. If $f$ approaches different numbers along two paths, the limit does not exist.

## partial derivative
links: 13.1#existence 17.3#partial-derivatives
$f_x(a, b) = \lim_{h\to0}\dfrac{f(a + h, b) - f(a, b)}{h}$, the derivative with respect to $x$ with $y$ held fixed; $f_y$ holds $x$ fixed. Also written $\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x}$ and $\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y}$.

## second partial derivatives
links: 17.3#higher
The partial derivatives $f_{xx}$, $f_{xy}$, $f_{yx}$ and $f_{yy}$ of $f_x$ and $f_y$. In $f_{xy}$ you differentiate first with respect to $x$, then $y$; $f_{xy}$ and $f_{yx}$ are the mixed partial derivatives.

## Clairaut's theorem
links: 17.3#higher
If $f$ is defined on a disc $D$ containing $(a, b)$, and $f_{xy}$ and $f_{yx}$ are both continuous on $D$, then $f_{xy}(a, b) = f_{yx}(a, b)$.

## partial differential equation
links: 17.3#higher 20.8#next
An equation between partial derivatives of an unknown function of several variables, such as the heat equation $u_t = k\,u_{xx}$, with $k > 0$, or Laplace's equation $u_{xx} + u_{yy} = 0$.

## tangent plane
links: 17.4#tangent-planes 17.6#tangent-planes-3d
If the partial derivatives of $f$ exist near $(a, b)$ and are continuous at $(a, b)$, the tangent plane to $z = f(x, y)$ there is $z = f(a, b) + f_x(a, b)(x - a) + f_y(a, b)(y - b)$. For a level surface $F = k$, it is the plane through the point with normal vector $\nabla F$, where $\nabla F \ne \vb{0}$.

## differentiable (two variables)
links: 17.4#differentiability
$f$ is differentiable at $(a, b)$ if $f_x$ and $f_y$ exist there and the error $E$ of the linearisation satisfies $E(x, y)/\sqrt{(x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2} \to 0$ as $(x, y) \to (a, b)$. Partial derivatives that exist on a disc around $(a, b)$ and are continuous at $(a, b)$ guarantee it.

## total differential
links: 17.4#differential
For differentiable $z = f(x, y)$: $dz = f_x\,dx + f_y\,dy = \dfrac{\partial z}{\partial x}\,dx + \dfrac{\partial z}{\partial y}\,dy$, where the differentials $dx$ and $dy$ are independent variables.

## tree diagram
links: 17.5#tree-diagrams
A diagram for the chain rule: the dependent variable at the top, the intermediate variables below it, the independent ones at the bottom, and each branch labelled with a derivative. The derivative of the top variable with respect to a bottom one is the sum, over all paths down to it, of the products of the labels.

## implicit function theorem
links: 17.5#implicit
If $F$ has continuous partial derivatives near $(a, b)$, $F(a, b) = 0$ and $F_y(a, b) \ne 0$, then near $(a, b)$ the curve $F(x, y) = 0$ is the graph of a differentiable function $y(x)$, with $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{F_x}{F_y}$.

## directional derivative
links: 17.6#gradient
For a unit vector $\vb{u} = \langle u_1, u_2\rangle$, $D_{\vb{u}} f(a, b) = \lim_{h\to0}\dfrac{f(a + hu_1, b + hu_2) - f(a, b)}{h}$: the rate of change of $f$ in the direction of $\vb{u}$. If $f$ is differentiable, it equals $\nabla f(a, b)\cdot\vb{u}$.

## gradient
links: 17.6#gradient
The vector of partial derivatives, $\nabla f = \langle f_x, f_y\rangle$, or $\langle f_x, f_y, f_z\rangle$ in three variables. Where it is not $\vb{0}$, it points in the direction of steepest ascent, its length is the largest rate of increase, and it is perpendicular to the level curve.

## steepest ascent
links: 17.6#steepest-ascent
For differentiable $f$ with $\nabla f(a, b) \ne \vb{0}$, the directional derivative is largest, $\norm{\nabla f}$, in the direction of $\nabla f$, smallest, $-\norm{\nabla f}$, in the opposite direction, and 0 perpendicular to it.

## computational graph
links: 17.8#backpropagation
A graph of a computation with a node for each input and each intermediate result, and an arrow from each node to each node computed directly from it: its children; the nodes it is computed from are its parents. Each arrow carries a local derivative.

## local derivative
links: 17.8#backpropagation
On an arrow of a computational graph, the partial derivative of the child with respect to the node the arrow comes from.

## adjoint
links: 17.8#backpropagation
In a computation that ends with the number $L$, the adjoint of a node $v$ is $\bar v = \partial L/\partial v$. The backward pass computes the adjoints from the output back to the inputs.

## forward pass
links: 17.8#backpropagation
In backpropagation, the computation of every node's value from the inputs to the output, keeping the values; the backward pass then computes the adjoints from the output back to the inputs.

## open disc
links: 18.1#critical-points
The open disc of radius $r > 0$ centred at $(a, b)$ is the set of points $(x, y)$ with $(x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 < r^2$.

## critical point
links: 8.1#extrema 18.1#critical-points
A point $(a, b)$ of the domain of $f$ where $\nabla f(a, b) = \vb{0}$, or where $f_x$ or $f_y$ does not exist. Every local extremum is at a critical point. Some books say critical point for the critical number of a function of one variable.

## saddle point
links: 18.1#critical-points
A critical point at which $f$ has neither a local maximum nor a local minimum: every open disc around it contains points where $f$ is larger and points where it is smaller, as the origin for $x^2 - y^2$.

## Hessian matrix
links: 18.2#second-derivative-test
The square array of the second partial derivatives of $f$ at a point, with $f_{xx}$, $f_{xy}$ in its first row and $f_{yx}$, $f_{yy}$ in its second; when $f_{xy} = f_{yx}$, its determinant is $D = f_{xx}f_{yy} - f_{xy}^2$.

## matrix
links: 18.2#second-derivative-test
A rectangular array of numbers.

## interior point
links: 18.3#absolute-extrema
A point $P$ of a set $R$ such that some open disc centred at $P$ lies in $R$.

## boundary point
links: 18.3#absolute-extrema
A point $P$ such that every open disc centred at $P$ contains points of $R$ and points not in $R$.

## closed set
links: 18.3#absolute-extrema
A set of the plane that contains all its boundary points.

## closed region method
links: 18.3#absolute-extrema
To find the absolute extreme values of a continuous $f$ on a closed, bounded region: find the values at the critical points inside, find the largest and smallest values on the boundary, and compare them all.

## constraint
links: 18.4#lagrange
An equation $g(x, y) = c$ that ties the variables of an optimisation problem together.

## Lagrange multipliers
links: 18.4#lagrange 18.4#two-constraints
If $f$, restricted to the curve $g(x, y) = c$, has a local extremum at a point where $\nabla g \ne \vb{0}$ (with continuous first partial derivatives), then $\nabla f = \lambda\nabla g$ there for some number $\lambda$, the Lagrange multiplier. With two constraints $g = c$ and $h = d$, $\nabla f = \lambda\nabla g + \mu\nabla h$.

## shadow price
links: 18.4#lagrange
The Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$ read as the rate at which the best value changes as the constraint is relaxed: what one more unit of budget is worth at the optimum.

## residual
links: 18.5#least-squares
For a line $y = mx + b$ and a data point $(x_i, y_i)$, the error $y_i - (mx_i + b)$.

## least squares
links: 18.5#least-squares
Fitting a line $y = mx + b$ to data points $(x_i, y_i)$ by minimising the sum of squared errors $E(m, b) = \sum (y_i - mx_i - b)^2$. The minimising line is the least-squares line, or regression line; it solves the normal equations and passes through the mean point $(\bar x, \bar y)$.

## normal equations
links: 18.5#least-squares
$\big(\sum x_i^2\big)m + \big(\sum x_i\big)b = \sum x_iy_i$ and $\big(\sum x_i\big)m + nb = \sum y_i$: the equations $\partial E/\partial m = \partial E/\partial b = 0$ for the least-squares line. With at least two different $x_i$ they have exactly one solution.

## ill-conditioned
links: 18.6#ill-conditioning
A function whose level curves near its minimum are long, thin ellipses (a large condition number): gradient descent zigzags and converges slowly on it.

## standardise
links: 18.6#rescaling
To shift data to mean 0 and scale it to a similar spread, as is done to a model's input features before training, so that gradient descent is not ill-conditioned.

## eigenvalues
links: 18.6#rescaling
For a bowl with tilted elliptical level curves, its curvatures along the axes of the ellipses: the eigenvalues of the Hessian matrix, which the Mathematical Foundations series studies.

## double integral
links: 19.1#double-integrals
$\iint_R f(x, y)\,dA$, the limit of double Riemann sums $\sum\sum f(x_{ij}^*, y_{ij}^*)\,\Delta A$ over a rectangle $R$, cut into $m \times n$ small rectangles of area $\Delta A$, when it exists. For $f \ge 0$ it is the volume under the surface $z = f(x, y)$.

## element of area
links: 19.1#double-integrals 19.4#polar
$dA$ in $\iint_R f\,dA$, which recalls the areas $\Delta A$ of small rectangles: $dA = dx\,dy$, or $r\,dr\,d\theta$ in polar coordinates.

## iterated integral
links: 19.2#iterated
An integral of an integral, such as $\int_a^b\int_c^d f(x, y)\,dy\,dx$, computed from the inside out: the inner integral treats the outer variable as a constant (partial integration).

## partial integration
links: 19.2#iterated
Integrating with respect to one variable while treating the other as a constant, as a partial derivative differentiates.

## Fubini's theorem
links: 19.2#iterated
If $f$ is continuous on the rectangle $R = [a, b] \times [c, d]$, then $\iint_R f\,dA = \int_a^b\int_c^d f\,dy\,dx = \int_c^d\int_a^b f\,dx\,dy$: either order of the iterated integral gives the double integral.

## type I region
links: 19.3#general-regions
A region $a \le x \le b$, $g_1(x) \le y \le g_2(x)$ between the graphs of two continuous functions of $x$; over it, $\iint_D f\,dA = \int_a^b\int_{g_1(x)}^{g_2(x)} f\,dy\,dx$. A type II region, $c \le y \le d$, $h_1(y) \le x \le h_2(y)$, lies between graphs of functions of $y$.

## reversing the order of integration
links: 19.3#general-regions
Rewriting an iterated integral over a region with the other order of integration, with new limits read from a sketch of the region; it can turn an impossible integral into an easy one.

## polar rectangle
links: 19.4#polar
A region $r_0 \le r \le r_1$, $\alpha \le \theta \le \beta$ in polar coordinates.

## moments of inertia
links: 19.5#inertia
For a lamina with density $\rho$: $I_x = \iint_D y^2\rho\,dA$ about the $x$-axis, $I_y = \iint_D x^2\rho\,dA$ about the $y$-axis, and $I_0 = I_x + I_y$ about the origin.

## joint density
links: 19.5#joint-densities
A function $f(x, y) \ge 0$ whose integral over the whole plane is 1. A pair of random variables $(X, Y)$ has joint density $f$ if $P\big((X, Y) \in D\big) = \iint_D f\,dA$ for every region $D$.

## independent random variables
links: 19.5#joint-densities
Random variables $X$ and $Y$, with densities $f_1$ and $f_2$, whose joint density is the product $f_1(x)\,f_2(y)$.

## Monte Carlo method
links: 19.5#joint-densities
Estimating an integral or a probability from many random trials. Its error shrinks only like $1/\sqrt{N}$, but it works in many dimensions, where grids need too many points.

## triple integral
links: 19.6#triple
$\iiint_E f(x, y, z)\,dV$, the limit of triple Riemann sums over small boxes of volume $\Delta V$, computed as an iterated integral in any of six orders. With $f = 1$ it is the volume of $E$.

## type 1 solid
links: 19.6#triple
A solid $u_1(x, y) \le z \le u_2(x, y)$ between two surfaces over a region $D$ of the $xy$-plane; its triple integral is $\iint_D \Big[\int_{u_1}^{u_2} f\,dz\Big]\,dA$.

## cylindrical coordinates
links: 19.7#cylindrical
$(r, \theta, z)$: the polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$ of a point's shadow on the $xy$-plane, and its height $z$. In them, $dV = r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta$.

## spherical coordinates
links: 19.7#spherical
$(\rho, \theta, \varphi)$: the distance $\rho \ge 0$ from the origin, the angle $\theta$ of cylindrical coordinates, and the angle $\varphi$ from the positive $z$-axis, $0 \le \varphi \le \pi$. Then $x = \rho\sin\varphi\cos\theta$, $y = \rho\sin\varphi\sin\theta$, $z = \rho\cos\varphi$, and $dV = \rho^2\sin\varphi\,d\rho\,d\theta\,d\varphi$.

## transformation
links: 19.8#change-of-variables
A function $T$ that sends each point $(u, v)$ of a region $S$ of the $uv$-plane to a point $(x, y) = T(u, v)$ of the $xy$-plane.

## Jacobian
links: 19.8#change-of-variables
For $x = x(u, v)$, $y = y(u, v)$ with continuous first partial derivatives, the determinant $\dfrac{\partial(x, y)}{\partial(u, v)} = x_uy_v - x_vy_u$: the factor by which the transformation multiplies small areas.

## change of variables
links: 19.8#change-of-variables
$\iint_D f(x, y)\,dA = \iint_S f\big(x(u, v), y(u, v)\big)\abs{\dfrac{\partial(x, y)}{\partial(u, v)}}\,du\,dv$, for a transformation with continuous first partial derivatives that maps $S$ onto $D$ one-to-one, with nonzero Jacobian, except perhaps on the boundary of $S$; $S$ and $D$ of type I or II.

## vector field
links: 20.1#fields
A function $\vb{F}$ that assigns a vector to each point of a region: $\vb{F}(x, y) = \langle P, Q\rangle$ in the plane, $\langle P, Q, R\rangle$ in space. A function whose values are numbers is then called a scalar field.

## source
links: 20.1#fields
A vector field, such as $\langle x, y\rangle$, whose flow streams outwards as if pumped in at a point; its opposite, such as $\langle -x, -y\rangle$, is a sink.

## inverse square field
links: 20.1#fields
A field such as gravity, $\vb{F} = -\dfrac{GMm}{\norm{\vb{r}}^3}\,\vb{r}$, that points towards the origin with length proportional to $1/\norm{\vb{r}}^2$: twice as far away, a quarter of the pull.

## gradient field
links: 20.1#fields
The vector field $\nabla f$ of a differentiable function $f$.

## flow line
links: 20.1#fields
A curve $\vb{r}(t)$ whose velocity is always the field at its position, $\vb{r}'(t) = \vb{F}\big(\vb{r}(t)\big)$: the path of a particle carried by the flow. Also called a streamline.

## smooth curve
links: 20.2#line-integrals
A curve traced by a smooth path with continuous velocity that is nonzero at interior parameter values. The series allows the velocity to vanish at an endpoint.

## line integral
links: 20.2#line-integrals 20.2#work
Of a function $f$ along a smooth curve $C$ traced by $\vb{r}(t)$, $a \le t \le b$: $\int_C f\,ds = \int_a^b f\big(\vb{r}(t)\big)\norm{\vb{r}'(t)}\,dt$. Of a vector field: $\int_C \vb{F}\cdot d\vb{r} = \int_a^b \vb{F}\big(\vb{r}(t)\big)\cdot\vb{r}'(t)\,dt$, the work done by a force $\vb{F}$; it changes sign when $C$ is traced the other way.

## fundamental theorem for line integrals
links: 20.3#conservative
If $f$ is differentiable with a continuous gradient on an open region that contains a piecewise smooth curve $C$ from $A$ to $B$, then $\int_C \nabla f\cdot d\vb{r} = f(B) - f(A)$.

## conservative field
links: 20.3#conservative
A vector field $\vb{F} = \nabla f$ for some function $f$, its potential. On an open connected region, a continuous field is conservative exactly when its line integrals are independent of the path, and exactly when they are 0 round every closed curve.

## potential function
links: 20.3#conservative
A function $f$ with $\nabla f = \vb{F}$; physicists call $U = -f$ the potential energy.

## independent of the path
links: 20.3#conservative
A line integral is independent of the path if it depends only on the two ends of the curve, as for every continuous conservative field.

## closed curve
links: 20.3#conservative
A curve that ends where it starts. $\oint_C$ marks a line integral round a closed curve.

## connected
links: 20.3#conservative
A region is connected if any two of its points can be joined by a piecewise smooth curve in it.

## open region
links: 20.3#conservative
A region with a disc (a ball, in space) around each of its points that lies in the region.

## simple curve
links: 20.3#test
A curve that does not cross itself; a simple closed curve meets itself only where it ends at its start.

## simply connected
links: 20.3#test 20.5#identities
A connected region of the plane in which every simple closed curve encloses only points of the region: it has no holes. In space, a region in which every closed curve can be shrunk to a point without leaving it.

## test for conservative fields
links: 20.3#test
If $P$ and $Q$ have continuous first partial derivatives on an open region, a conservative $\langle P, Q\rangle$ has $P_y = Q_x$; on an open, simply connected region, $P_y = Q_x$ also makes $\langle P, Q\rangle$ conservative.

## positively oriented
links: 20.4#green 20.7#stokes
A simple closed curve in the plane is positively oriented if it is traced anticlockwise, with the region it encloses on the left. The boundary of an oriented surface is positively oriented if the surface is on your left as you walk along it with your head along $\vb{n}$.

## Green's theorem
links: 20.4#green 20.4#flux-form
For a positively oriented, piecewise smooth, simple closed curve $C$ enclosing $D$, and $P$, $Q$ with continuous first partial derivatives on an open region containing $D$: $\oint_C P\,dx + Q\,dy = \iint_D (Q_x - P_y)\,dA$. Its flux form is $\oint_C \vb{F}\cdot\vb{n}\,ds = \iint_D (P_x + Q_y)\,dA$.

## circulation
links: 20.4#flux-form
$\oint_C \vb{F}\cdot\vb{T}\,ds = \oint_C \vb{F}\cdot d\vb{r}$: how strongly a field flows round a closed curve $C$.

## flux
links: 20.4#flux-form 20.6#flux
The rate at which a field flows across a curve or surface. Across a closed curve $C$ in the plane, $\oint_C \vb{F}\cdot\vb{n}\,ds$ with $\vb{n}$ the outward unit normal; across an oriented surface $S$, $\iint_S \vb{F}\cdot d\vb{S} = \iint_S \vb{F}\cdot\vb{n}\,dS$.

## outward unit normal
links: 20.4#flux-form 20.6#flux
For a curve traced anticlockwise in the plane, the unit tangent turned a quarter turn clockwise, pointing out of the region; for a closed surface, the unit normal that points out of the solid.

## shoelace formula
links: 20.4#area
The area of a polygon with vertices $(x_1, y_1), \dots, (x_n, y_n)$, in order anticlockwise, from Green's theorem: $\tfrac12\sum (x_iy_{i+1} - x_{i+1}y_i)$, with $(x_{n+1}, y_{n+1}) = (x_1, y_1)$. Also called the surveyor's formula.

## curl
links: 20.5#curl-divergence 20.5#meaning
$\operatorname{curl}\vb{F} = \nabla\times\vb{F} = \langle R_y - Q_z,\ P_z - R_x,\ Q_x - P_y\rangle$ for $\vb{F} = \langle P, Q, R\rangle$: the circulation per unit area round small loops, how strongly the field turns about each point.

## divergence
links: 20.5#curl-divergence 20.5#meaning
$\operatorname{div}\vb{F} = \nabla\cdot\vb{F} = P_x + Q_y + R_z$: the rate at which the flow carries fluid out of small regions, per unit of volume (of area, in the plane). Where it is positive the flow spreads out; where it is negative, it gathers.

## parametric surface
links: 20.6#parametric-surfaces
The set of points $\vb{r}(u, v) = \langle x(u, v), y(u, v), z(u, v)\rangle$ for $(u, v)$ in a region $D$ of the $uv$-plane. The parametrisation is smooth if $\vb{r}_u$ and $\vb{r}_v$ are continuous and $\vb{r}_u\times\vb{r}_v \ne \vb{0}$, except perhaps on the edge of $D$.

## grid curve
links: 20.6#parametric-surfaces
A curve on a parametric surface along which one of $u$ and $v$ is fixed; its tangent vector is $\vb{r}_u$ or $\vb{r}_v$.

## surface area
links: 20.6#surface-area
For a surface traced once by a smooth parametrisation $\vb{r}(u, v)$, $(u, v)$ in $D$: $A(S) = \iint_D \norm{\vb{r}_u\times\vb{r}_v}\,dA$.

## surface integral
links: 20.6#surface-integrals
$\iint_S f\,dS = \iint_D f\big(\vb{r}(u, v)\big)\norm{\vb{r}_u\times\vb{r}_v}\,dA$, for $f$ continuous on a surface $S$ traced once by a smooth parametrisation: $f$ times small areas $dS$, added up.

## oriented surface
links: 20.6#flux
A surface with a choice of unit normal $\vb{n}$ at each point, varying continuously over it, which says which side counts as positive. A closed surface, the whole boundary of a solid, is usually given the outward orientation.

## Stokes' theorem
links: 20.7#stokes
For an oriented piecewise smooth surface $S$ with a simple closed piecewise smooth boundary $C$ oriented by the right-hand rule, $\oint_C\vb{F}\cdot d\vb{r}=\iint_S\operatorname{curl}\vb{F}\cdot d\vb{S}$ if $\vb{F}$ has continuous first partial derivatives on an open neighbourhood of $S$.

## divergence theorem
links: 20.7#divergence-theorem
For a bounded solid $E$ with piecewise smooth outward-oriented boundary $S$, $\iint_S\vb{F}\cdot d\vb{S}=\iiint_E\operatorname{div}\vb{F}\,dV$ if $\vb{F}$ has continuous first partial derivatives on an open neighbourhood of the solid, including its boundary.

## the one pattern
sort: one pattern
links: 20.8#pattern
What every fundamental theorem of the series says: adding up a derivative over a region gives the values on the region's boundary. The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, the fundamental theorem for line integrals, Green's, Stokes' and the divergence theorem are all cases.

## differential forms
links: 20.8#next
The language in which the fundamental theorems of the series become one theorem, $\int_M d\omega = \int_{\partial M}\omega$.

## real analysis
links: 20.8#next
The study that proves, with $\varepsilon$–$\delta$ reasoning, the results this series quoted, such as the Extreme Value Theorem and the integrability of continuous functions.

## complex analysis
links: 20.8#next
Calculus with functions of a complex variable, the setting of Euler's formula.

## differential geometry
links: 20.8#next
The study of curves and surfaces with calculus: how they bend, and the shortest paths on them.

## bootstrap sample
links: ex7.8
A sample of $n$ items drawn at random, one at a time with replacement, from a data set of $n$ items, as statistics and machine learning use.

## mean squared error
links: ex8.9
The average of the squares of a model's errors on its data points: a common loss in machine learning.

## maximum power transfer theorem
links: ex8.7
A source delivers the most power to a load whose resistance matches its own.

## level set
links: 17.1#level-curves
The set of inputs where a function has a given value. Depending on the function and value it may be a curve, a surface, a point or another set.

## second derivative test in two variables
links: 18.2#second-derivative-test
At a stationary point with continuous second partials, a negative Hessian determinant gives a saddle. A positive determinant gives a local minimum if $f_{xx}>0$ or a local maximum if $f_{xx}<0$. A zero determinant leaves the test inconclusive.

## type II region
links: 19.3#general-regions
A planar region between two continuous functions of $y$ over a closed interval of $y$, with the left function at most the right one.

## spherical wedge
links: 19.7#spherical
A solid cut out by intervals for the spherical radius and the two spherical angles.

## smooth path
links: 20.2#line-integrals
A parametrised path on a closed interval with continuous velocity that is nonzero in the interior. The velocity may vanish at an endpoint.

## piecewise smooth path
links: 20.2#line-integrals
A path made of finitely many smooth pieces joined end to end, each with its own parameter interval.

## smooth patch
links: 20.6#parametric-surfaces
A surface patch with a continuously differentiable parametrisation on a neighbourhood of its parameter region, with the two parameter tangent vectors having nonzero cross product at interior points.
