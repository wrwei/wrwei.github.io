#! Modules 1 to 3

## rate of change
links: 1.1#about 1.6#rates 4.2#derivative
How fast one quantity changes as another changes, such as a speed. Over an interval it is the average rate of change; at an instant it is the instantaneous rate of change, the derivative.

## accumulation
links: 1.1#about 10.3#area-function
How much of a quantity has built up over a stretch of time when its rate keeps changing. Integration computes it.

## differentiation
links: 1.1#about 4.3#derivative-function
Finding a derivative. With integration it is one of the two processes of calculus, and the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus says that each undoes the other.

## integration
links: 1.1#about 10.6#indefinite
Finding an integral: to integrate $f$ is to find its definite integral or its indefinite integral.

## secant line
links: 1.1#two-problems 1.6#rates
The line through two points of a curve. For $y = f(x)$ and the points at $a$ and $b$, its slope is the average rate of change $\dfrac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}$.

## tangent line
links: 1.1#two-problems 4.1#tangent-line
The line that secant lines through a point $P$ of a curve settle on as the second point approaches $P$. For $y = f(x)$ at $P = (a, f(a))$, when $f'(a)$ exists, it is $y = f(a) + f'(a)(x - a)$.

## limit
links: 1.1#two-problems 2.1#idea 2.7#precise 17.2#limits
$\lim_{x\to a} f(x) = L$ if $f(x)$ can be made as close to $L$ as you like by taking $x$ close enough to $a$, but not equal to $a$; $f$ must be defined on an open interval around $a$, except possibly at $a$. Section 2.7 makes this precise with $\varepsilon$ and $\delta$; Section 17.2 extends it to two variables.

## derivative
links: 1.1#two-problems 4.2#derivative 4.3#derivative-function
The derivative of $f$ at $a$ is $f'(a) = \lim_{h\to0}\dfrac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h}$, when this limit exists as a real number: the slope of the tangent line and the instantaneous rate of change. The derivative function $f'$ takes this value at every $x$ where $f$ is differentiable.

## integral
links: 1.1#two-problems 9.4#definite-integral 10.6#indefinite
The area under a curve, found as the limit of sums of thin rectangles; made precise as the definite integral. The indefinite integral is the general antiderivative.

## gradient descent
links: 1.1#uses 8.7#gradient-descent 17.7#gradient-descent 18.6#gradient-descent
A method for minimising a differentiable function: from a starting point, repeat $x_{k+1} = x_k - \eta f'(x_k)$ in one variable, or $\vb{x}_{k+1} = \vb{x}_k - \eta\nabla f(\vb{x}_k)$ in several, with a step size $\eta > 0$. Machine learning trains models with it.

## function
links: 1.3#functions
A rule $f$ that assigns to each number $x$ in a set $D$, its domain, exactly one number $f(x)$.

## domain
links: 1.3#functions 17.1#functions
The set of inputs of a function. For a formula with no domain stated, the convention is the set of all real numbers for which the formula gives a real number.

## value
links: 1.3#functions
The number $f(x)$ that a function $f$ assigns to $x$: the value of $f$ at $x$.

## range
links: 1.3#functions 17.1#functions 16.8#projectiles
(1) The set of all values of a function as its input runs through the domain. (2) The horizontal distance a projectile travels on level ground before it lands.

## independent variable
links: 1.3#functions 17.1#functions
The input of a function: $x$ in $y = f(x)$, or $x$ and $y$ in $z = f(x, y)$.

## dependent variable
links: 1.3#functions 17.1#functions
The output of a function: $y$ in $y = f(x)$, or $z$ in $z = f(x, y)$.

## interval
links: 1.3#intervals
A set of real numbers such as $[a, b]$, $(a, b)$, $[a, b)$ or $[a, \infty)$: all the numbers between two ends, or beyond one end. A square bracket means that the endpoint belongs to the interval, a round one that it does not.

## closed interval
links: 1.3#intervals
$[a, b]$, for $a < b$: the numbers $x$ with $a \le x \le b$, both endpoints included.

## open interval
links: 1.3#intervals
$(a, b)$, for $a < b$: the numbers $x$ with $a < x < b$, neither endpoint included.

## graph
links: 1.3#graphs 17.1#graphs
The set of points $(x, f(x))$ with $x$ in the domain of $f$: the curve $y = f(x)$. For a function of two variables, the points $(x, y, f(x, y))$: a surface in space.

## vertical line test
links: 1.3#graphs
A curve in the plane is the graph of a function of $x$ exactly when no vertical line meets it more than once.

## piecewise function
links: 1.3#piecewise
A function that uses different formulas on different parts of its domain, such as $\abs{x}$.

## absolute value
links: 1.3#piecewise
$\abs{x} = x$ if $x \ge 0$ and $\abs{x} = -x$ if $x < 0$: the distance from $x$ to 0. $\abs{a - b}$ is the distance between $a$ and $b$.

## rectified linear unit (ReLU)
links: 1.3#piecewise
The piecewise function $\operatorname{ReLU}(x) = \max(0, x)$: 0 for $x < 0$ and $x$ for $x \ge 0$. Neural networks use it.

## pure function
links: 1.3#piecewise
A function, in a program, whose output depends only on its input and which does nothing else. Mathematical functions are pure.

## linear function
links: 1.4#linear
A function $f(x) = mx + b$ with constants $m$ and $b$. Its graph is a straight line with slope $m$ and intercept $b$.

## slope
links: 1.4#linear
The change in $y$ per unit change in $x$ along a line: $m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$ for any two of its points with $x_1 \ne x_2$. The slope of a curve at a point is the slope of its tangent line there.

## intercept
links: 1.4#linear
The height $b$ at which the line $y = mx + b$ crosses the $y$-axis.

## point-slope equation
links: 1.4#linear
$y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$: the line through $(x_1, y_1)$ with slope $m$.

## power function
links: 1.4#polynomials
A function $f(x) = x^n$. Negative powers are reciprocals, $x^{-n} = 1/x^n$, defined for $x \ne 0$; fractional powers are roots.

## even function
links: 1.4#polynomials 9.6#symmetry
A function with $f(-x) = f(x)$ for every $x$ in its domain. Its graph is its own mirror image in the $y$-axis, and $\int_{-a}^{a} f = 2\int_0^a f$.

## odd function
links: 1.4#polynomials 9.6#symmetry
A function with $f(-x) = -f(x)$ for every $x$ in its domain. A half turn about the origin leaves its graph unchanged, and $\int_{-a}^{a} f = 0$.

## polynomial
links: 1.4#polynomials
A function $p(x) = a_nx^n + \dots + a_1x + a_0$, where $n \ge 0$ is a whole number and the coefficients $a_0, \dots, a_n$ are constants. Its domain is $\R$.

## coefficient
links: 1.4#polynomials 15.1#power-series
A constant that multiplies a power of the variable: $a_k$ in a polynomial $\sum a_kx^k$, or $c_n$ in a power series $\sum c_n(x - a)^n$.

## degree
links: 1.4#polynomials
A polynomial $a_nx^n + \dots + a_0$ with $a_n \ne 0$ has degree $n$.

## quadratic
links: 1.4#polynomials
A polynomial of degree 2, $ax^2 + bx + c$ with $a \ne 0$.

## cubic
links: 1.4#polynomials
A polynomial of degree 3.

## zero (of a function)
links: 1.4#polynomials
A number $r$ with $p(r) = 0$, also called a root; there the graph meets the $x$-axis.

## rational function
links: 1.4#rational
A quotient $\dfrac{p(x)}{q(x)}$ of two polynomials, where $q$ is not the zero polynomial. Its domain is the set of all $x$ with $q(x) \ne 0$.

## square root
links: 1.4#roots
For $x \ge 0$, $\sqrt{x}$ is the non-negative number whose square is $x$. So $\sqrt{x^2} = \abs{x}$.

## cube root
links: 1.4#roots
$\sqrt[3]{x}$, the number whose cube is $x$, defined for every real $x$. The $n$th root $\sqrt[n]{x} = x^{1/n}$ is defined for every $x$ when $n$ is odd and for $x \ge 0$ when $n$ is even.

## exponential function
links: 1.4#exponential
A function $a^x$ with a fixed base $a > 0$. For $a \ne 1$ its domain is $\R$ and its range $(0, \infty)$; *the* exponential function is $e^x$.

## Euler's number
links: 1.4#exponential 6.1#base-e 6.3#two-routes
$e = 2.718281828\ldots$, the base whose exponential has slope 1 at $x = 0$: $\lim_{h\to0}\dfrac{e^h - 1}{h} = 1$. Also $e = \lim_{n\to\infty}(1 + 1/n)^n$.

## logarithm
links: 1.4#logarithms
For $a > 0$ with $a \ne 1$ and $x > 0$, $\log_a x$ is the number $y$ with $a^y = x$: $\log_a$ is the inverse of $a^x$.

## natural logarithm
links: 1.4#logarithms 6.1#base-e
$\ln x$, the logarithm to base $e$: for $x > 0$, $\ln x = y$ means $e^y = x$. In Python, `math.log` and SymPy's `log` are $\ln$.

## laws of logarithms
links: 1.4#logarithms
For $x > 0$, $y > 0$ and every real $r$: $\ln(xy) = \ln x + \ln y$, $\ln(x/y) = \ln x - \ln y$ and $\ln(x^r) = r\ln x$; the same for $\log_a$.

## unit circle
links: 1.4#trig
The circle of radius 1 centred at the origin.

## radian
links: 1.4#trig
The unit of angle in calculus: an angle at the centre of the unit circle that cuts off an arc of length $\theta$ measures $\theta$ radians. So $180^\circ = \pi$ radians.

## sine and cosine
links: 1.4#trig
Turn from $(1, 0)$ through $\theta$ radians round the unit circle to a point $P$: then $\cos\theta$ is the $x$-coordinate of $P$ and $\sin\theta$ its $y$-coordinate. Both have domain $\R$ and range $[-1, 1]$.

## trigonometric functions
links: 1.4#trig
Sine and cosine, and the four defined from them where the denominator is not 0: $\tan\theta = \sin\theta/\cos\theta$, $\cot\theta = \cos\theta/\sin\theta$, $\sec\theta = 1/\cos\theta$ and $\csc\theta = 1/\sin\theta$.

## Pythagorean identity
links: 1.4#trig
$\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$ for every $\theta$, because $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ lies on the unit circle.

## periodic
links: 1.4#trig
Repeating at regular steps: $\sin(\theta + 2\pi) = \sin\theta$ and $\cos(\theta + 2\pi) = \cos\theta$, so sine and cosine are periodic with period $2\pi$; the tangent has period $\pi$.

## addition formulas
links: 1.4#trig
$\sin(x + y) = \sin x\cos y + \cos x\sin y$ and $\cos(x + y) = \cos x\cos y - \sin x\sin y$.

## composition
links: 1.5#composition
$f \circ g$, the function $(f \circ g)(x) = f\big(g(x)\big)$. Its domain is the set of $x$ in the domain of $g$ for which $g(x)$ is in the domain of $f$.

## inner function
links: 1.5#composition
In a composition $f\big(g(x)\big)$, the function $g$ applied first; $f$ is the outer function.

## outer function
links: 1.5#composition
In a composition $f\big(g(x)\big)$, the function $f$ applied last, to the value of the inner function $g$.

## one-to-one
links: 1.5#inverse
A function is one-to-one if different inputs always give different outputs: $x_1 \ne x_2$ implies $f(x_1) \ne f(x_2)$.

## horizontal line test
links: 1.5#inverse
A function is one-to-one exactly when no horizontal line meets its graph more than once.

## inverse function
links: 1.5#inverse 6.2#inverse-functions
For a one-to-one $f$ with domain $D$ and range $R$, the function $f^{-1}$ with domain $R$ and range $D$ such that $f^{-1}(y) = x$ exactly when $f(x) = y$. Its graph is the reflection of the graph of $f$ in the line $y = x$.

## restricting the domain
links: 1.5#inverse
Cutting down the domain of a function that is not one-to-one so that it becomes one-to-one, as $x^2$ on $[0, \infty)$, whose inverse is $\sqrt{x}$.

## average rate of change
links: 1.6#rates
The average rate of change of $f$ over $[a, b]$ is $\dfrac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a} = \dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$, the slope of the secant line through $(a, f(a))$ and $(b, f(b))$.

## difference quotient
links: 1.6#difference-quotient
$\dfrac{f(a + h) - f(a)}{h}$, for $h \ne 0$ with $a$ and $a + h$ in the domain of $f$: the average rate of change of $f$ between $a$ and $a + h$.

## instantaneous speed
links: 1.6#shrinking 4.1#tangents
The speed at one instant: the number that average speeds over shorter and shorter time intervals approach. Module 4 defines it as the absolute value of the instantaneous velocity.

## SymPy
links: 1.7#python
A free Python package for exact algebra and calculus with symbols.

## NumPy
links: 1.7#python
A free Python package for computing with arrays of numbers.

## matplotlib
links: 1.7#python
A free Python package for plotting graphs.

## virtual environment
links: 1.7#venv
A folder with its own set of Python packages, kept apart from the rest of the computer: packages installed while it is active go into that folder.

## symbol (SymPy)
links: 1.7#first-session
A name for an unknown number in SymPy, created with `symbols`, so that formulas can be manipulated exactly.

## floating-point number
links: 1.7#first-session 2.8#cancellation
A computer's fast approximation to a real number, stored in binary and rounded to about 16 significant decimal digits.

## array
links: 1.7#first-session
In NumPy, a list of numbers that arithmetic acts on all at once.

## left-hand limit
links: 2.2#one-sided
$\lim_{x\to a^-} f(x) = L$: $f(x)$ can be made as close to $L$ as you like by taking $x < a$ close enough to $a$; $f$ must be defined on an interval $(c, a)$. Also called the limit from the left.

## right-hand limit
links: 2.2#one-sided
$\lim_{x\to a^+} f(x) = L$: the same as a left-hand limit, with $x > a$ and $f$ defined on an interval $(a, d)$. Also called the limit from the right.

## one-sided limit
links: 2.2#one-sided 2.7#no-limit-proof
A left-hand or right-hand limit. $\lim_{x\to a} f(x) = L$ exactly when both one-sided limits at $a$ exist and equal $L$.

## does not exist (limit)
links: 2.1#idea 2.3#no-limit
A limit does not exist when no number $L$ fits the definition, as with a jump, a blow-up or an oscillation. An infinite limit does not exist in this sense either.

## unit step function
links: 2.2#one-sided 4.4#failures
The function that is 0 for negative inputs and 1 for positive ones, written $u(t)$ in Module 2, where its value at 0 is left open, and $H(x)$ with $H(0) = 1$ in Module 4. It jumps at 0, so it is not continuous or differentiable there.

## infinite limit
links: 2.3#no-limit 3.6#infinite-limits
$\lim_{x\to a} f(x) = \infty$ means that for every number $M$, $f(x) > M$ for every $x \ne a$ close enough to $a$; $-\infty$ likewise. Such a limit does not exist as a number: $\infty$ describes how it fails.

## limit laws
links: 2.4#laws 14.1#laws
If $\lim f = L$ and $\lim g = M$, the limits of $f + g$, $f - g$, $cf$, $fg$, $f/g$ (when $M \ne 0$), $f^n$ and $\sqrt[n]{f}$ are $L + M$, $L - M$, $cL$, $LM$, $L/M$, $L^n$ and $\sqrt[n]{L}$ (for even $n$, if $L > 0$). Sequences obey the same laws.

## direct substitution
links: 2.4#substitution
Finding a limit by evaluating: $\lim_{x\to a} p(x) = p(a)$ for a polynomial $p$, and $\lim_{x\to a} r(x) = r(a)$ for a rational function $r$ with $r(a)$ defined. It works for every continuous function.

## indeterminate form
links: 2.5#zero-over-zero 7.5#lhopital 7.6#other-forms
A form whose limit the form alone does not decide: $0/0$, $\infty/\infty$, $0\cdot\infty$, $\infty - \infty$, $1^\infty$, $0^0$ and $\infty^0$. L'Hôpital's rule handles the first two, and the others are turned into them.

## conjugate
links: 2.5#rationalise
The same two terms with the opposite sign between them: $\sqrt{x} + 3$ is the conjugate of $\sqrt{x} - 3$. Multiplying a fraction above and below by it rationalises a difference that contains a square root.

## squeeze theorem
links: 2.6#squeeze 14.1#laws 17.2#squeeze
If $g(x) \le f(x) \le h(x)$ for every $x \ne a$ in an open interval around $a$, and $g$ and $h$ have the same limit $L$ at $a$, then $\lim_{x\to a} f(x) = L$. Module 14 states it for sequences and Module 17 for two variables.

## small-angle approximation
links: 2.6#trig-limits 7.2#how-good
$\sin\theta \approx \theta$ for $\theta$ near 0, in radians, because $\sin\theta/\theta \to 1$. It is the linearisation of $\sin$ at 0.

## ε–δ definition of a limit
sort: epsilon delta definition of a limit
links: 2.7#precise 2.7#proofs
$\lim_{x\to a} f(x) = L$ means: for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a $\delta > 0$ such that $\abs{f(x) - L} < \varepsilon$ for every $x$ with $0 < \abs{x - a} < \delta$; $f$ must be defined on an open interval around $a$, except possibly at $a$.

## scratch work
links: 2.7#proofs
In an $\varepsilon$–$\delta$ proof, the working backwards from $\abs{f(x) - L} < \varepsilon$ to a condition on $\abs{x - a}$ that finds $\delta$. The proof then runs the argument forwards.

## triangle inequality
links: 2.7#no-limit-proof 16.4#arithmetic
$\abs{a + b} \le \abs{a} + \abs{b}$ for all real numbers $a$ and $b$. For vectors, $\norm{\vb{a} + \vb{b}} \le \norm{\vb{a}} + \norm{\vb{b}}$.

## cancellation error
links: 2.8#cancellation
The loss of accuracy when a computer subtracts two nearly equal floating-point numbers: if they agree in their first $k$ digits, about $k$ of the 16 significant digits are lost.

## continuous
links: 3.1#continuity 3.1#one-sided 17.2#continuity
$f$ is continuous at $a$ if $\lim_{x\to a} f(x) = f(a)$: $f(a)$ is defined, the limit exists and the two are equal. It is continuous on an interval if it is continuous at every number of the interval, except that at an endpoint that belongs to it, it need only be continuous from the side of the interval. Module 17 defines continuity in two variables.

## discontinuity
links: 3.1#continuity 3.1#kinds
A number $a$ at which $f$ is not continuous, though $f$ is defined on an open interval around $a$ except perhaps at $a$; $f$ is then discontinuous at $a$. Its kinds are removable, jump, infinite and oscillating.

## continuous from the right
links: 3.1#one-sided
$f$ is continuous from the right at $a$ if $\lim_{x\to a^+} f(x) = f(a)$, and continuous from the left if $\lim_{x\to a^-} f(x) = f(a)$.

## floor function
links: 3.1#one-sided
$\lfloor x\rfloor$, the largest integer less than or equal to $x$: $\lfloor 2.7\rfloor = 2$ and $\lfloor -2.7\rfloor = -3$.

## removable discontinuity
links: 3.1#kinds
A discontinuity at $a$ where $\lim_{x\to a} f(x)$ exists. Redefining $f(a)$ as the limit makes $f$ continuous at $a$.

## jump discontinuity
links: 3.1#kinds
A discontinuity at which both one-sided limits exist but are different.

## infinite discontinuity
links: 3.1#kinds
A discontinuity at $a$ where, from at least one side, $f(x)$ becomes and stays larger than any number you name, or smaller than any: an infinite one-sided limit.

## oscillating discontinuity
links: 3.1#kinds
A discontinuity that is not removable, a jump or infinite: at least one one-sided limit fails to exist without becoming infinite, as when $\sin\big(1/(x - 2)\big)$ swings between $-1$ and 1 near 2.

## sinc function
links: 3.1#kinds
$\dfrac{\sin x}{x}$ for $x \ne 0$, with the value 1 at 0, which makes it continuous; a function of signal processing. Some books put $\pi x$ in place of $x$.

## continuity laws
links: 3.2#laws
If $f$ and $g$ are continuous at $a$, so are $f + g$, $f - g$, $cf$, $fg$, and $f/g$ if $g(a) \ne 0$. Compositions of continuous functions are continuous.

## Intermediate Value Theorem
links: 3.3#ivt
If $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$ and $N$ is any number strictly between $f(a)$ and $f(b)$, then $f(c) = N$ for at least one $c$ in $(a, b)$.

## completeness
links: 3.3#ivt 14.2#mct
The property of the real numbers that the number line has no gaps. The proofs of the Intermediate Value Theorem and of the Monotone Convergence Theorem rest on it; analysis states it as the least upper bound property.

## bisection method
links: 3.4#bisection
A way to find a root of a continuous $f$ on $[a, b]$ with $f(a)$ and $f(b)$ of opposite signs: halve the bracket again and again, keeping the half where the sign changes. After $n$ steps the midpoint is within $(b - a)/2^{n+1}$ of a root.

## bracket
links: 3.4#bisection
In the bisection method, an interval $[a_n, b_n]$ at whose ends $f$ has opposite signs, so that it contains a root.

## maximum value
links: 3.5#evt 8.1#extrema
$f(c)$ is the maximum value of $f$ on a set $I$ if $c$ is in $I$ and $f(x) \le f(c)$ for every $x$ in $I$; the minimum value $f(d)$ has $f(x) \ge f(d)$ instead.

## minimum value
links: 3.5#evt 8.1#extrema
$f(d)$ is the minimum value of $f$ on a set $I$ if $d$ is in $I$ and $f(x) \ge f(d)$ for every $x$ in $I$.

## extreme value
links: 3.5#evt 8.1#extrema 18.1#critical-points
A maximum or minimum value, absolute or local; also called an extremum (plural extrema).

## Extreme Value Theorem
links: 3.5#evt 18.3#absolute-extrema
A function continuous on a closed interval $[a, b]$ has a maximum value and a minimum value there. In two variables: a function continuous on a closed, bounded set has an absolute maximum and an absolute minimum on it.

## vertical asymptote
links: 3.6#infinite-limits
The line $x = a$ is a vertical asymptote of the graph of $f$ if at least one of the one-sided limits of $f$ at $a$ is $\infty$ or $-\infty$.

## limit at infinity
links: 3.7#limits-at-infinity
$\lim_{x\to\infty} f(x) = L$ means that $f(x)$ can be made as close to $L$ as you like by taking $x$ large enough; $f$ must be defined on an interval $(c, \infty)$. For $x \to -\infty$, $x$ is negative and large in size.

## horizontal asymptote
links: 3.7#limits-at-infinity
The line $y = L$ is a horizontal asymptote of the graph of $f$ if $\lim_{x\to\infty} f(x) = L$ or $\lim_{x\to-\infty} f(x) = L$.

## oblique asymptote
links: 3.7#oblique
The line $y = mx + c$, with $m \ne 0$, is an oblique (or slant) asymptote of the graph of $f$ if $f(x) - (mx + c) \to 0$ as $x \to \infty$ or as $x \to -\infty$.

## grows more slowly
links: 3.8#growth 7.7#growth
For functions positive for all large $x$, $f$ grows more slowly than $g$, written $f \ll g$, if $\lim_{x\to\infty} f(x)/g(x) = 0$. For every $p > 0$ and $a > 1$, $\ln x \ll x^p \ll a^x$, and $a^n \ll n! \ll n^n$.

## big-O notation
links: 3.8#big-o 4.7#truncation
$f(n) = O\big(g(n)\big)$ as $n \to \infty$ if there are constants $C$ and $N$ with $\abs{f(n)} \le C\abs{g(n)}$ for every $n \ge N$. As $h \to 0$, $E(h) = O(h^p)$ if $\abs{E(h)} \le C\abs{h}^p$ whenever $0 < \abs{h} < \delta$, for some constants $C$ and $\delta > 0$.

## factorial
links: 3.8#growth
$n! = 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdots n$, the number of ways to put $n$ objects in a row; $0! = 1$.
